What is Baptism?

Baptism and church membership are intended to be embraced together. Baptism is a church-life act that exemplifies the believer’s connection to Christ and His Body, the Church. Having a right understanding of baptism means having a right understanding of the importance of the church. When individuals are baptized here, they are baptized into the family of Salem, committing to gather together to worship, engage one another for growth and encouragement, and reach together into this city and this world with the hope of the Gospel. Baptism is all about identification with Christ and commitment to His Church.

Since the earliest days of Christianity, baptism has been of utmost importance. More than a mere ritual, it signifies a profound commitment, an act of obedience where believers identify publicly with the Savior, Jesus Christ, and His Body, the local church. The waters of baptism powerfully symbolize the reality of salvation by faith in Christ alone. Just as water buries the old self, baptism symbolizes the burial of sin and the resurrection of new life in Christ.

At Salem, we believe the ordinance of baptism is a voluntary act of public testimony for regenerate believers who testify of their salvation. Through immersion, they identify with Christ's death, burial, and resurrection and unite with the local church. Baptism is a community-forming event and should be celebrated during corporate worship.

Baptism is not just something reserved for the spiritual elite. Rather, baptism is:

  • Commanded by Christ Himself (Matthew 28:18-20)
  • The practice and expectation of the early Church (Acts 2:38, 41, 9:18, 16:15)
  • A public proclamation of faith, forgiveness, and union with Christ and His Church (Romans 6:3-4)

Baptism is not a means of salvation but a public declaration and celebration of saving faith. The command to be baptized, along with the example of the New Testament and early church, underscores the importance of this act of obedience for every follower of Christ. Through baptism, believers publicly affirm their identification with Jesus. Baptism is a powerful representation, not of what the believer receives, but of the saving work of God in them. We believe water baptism by full immersion best expresses what baptism symbolizes — identification with the death, burial, and resurrection of Christ — as described by the Apostle Paul:

Or do you not know that all of us who were baptized into Christ Jesus were baptized into His death? Therefore we were buried with Him through baptism into death, so that as Christ was raised from the dead through the glory of the Father, so we too might walk in newness of life” — Romans 6:3-4

Baptism and church membership are intrinsically linked. Baptism, a pivotal act within the life of the church, signifies a believer's profound connection to Christ and their integration into the Body of Christ. A true understanding of baptism necessitates an appreciation for the vital role of the church community. When an individual is baptized at Salem, they are welcomed into our church family, committing to actively participate in our shared life: gathering for worship, engaging in meaningful relationships for mutual growth and encouragement, and reaching out together to share the hope of the gospel with our city and the world. At its core, baptism is a powerful statement of identification with Christ and a commitment to His Church.
