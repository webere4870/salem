Distinctives

Our Distinctives

Every church operates based on core convictions, whether explicitly stated or implicitly understood. At Salem New Beginnings Church, our foundational distinctives are: a high view of God and a high view of Scripture.

High View of God
Summary

While God is intimately accessible to us as our Father, Scripture also reveals Him as the transcendent Sovereign, exalted above all. We must approach God with profound reverence and respect, never taking Him lightly. God's transcendence is reflected in His holiness, encompassing both His moral purity and His majestic authority. He is distinct from and exalted above all else in the universe. Our highest aspiration in worship is to acknowledge and celebrate God's transcendence. We desire to worship Him with reverence and awe, recognizing His majesty and separateness. Only by understanding God's transcendence can we truly appreciate the profound grace of the Gospel, which reconciles us to such a magnificent and awe-inspiring God.


Chief Implications

A high view of God compels us to emphasize several necessary implications.


God's Sovereignty in All Things

We reject theologies that suggest man or even Satan has ultimate control. We believe God is absolutely sovereign over all of creation, as declared in Psalm 103:19 Yahweh has established His throne in the heavens, And His kingdom rules over all. God possesses supreme and unrestricted power and freedom to act. Sovereignty is inherent in God's very nature—He rules simply because He is God. Providence refers to the active outworking of God's rule, whereby He governs every detail of His creation.


God's Sovereignty in Salvation

Our high view of God leads us to believe that salvation is entirely God's work. God alone initiates and accomplishes spiritual regeneration, a miraculous act of new life imparted by the Holy Spirit through the Word of God. Scripture describes this divine work using powerful metaphors: resurrection from spiritual death, new creation, and spiritual birth.


Godward Worship

While we benefit greatly from gathering for worship, our primary purpose in worship is to glorify God. Our services are therefore designed to center on Him.

High View of Scripture
A Summary

Our profound respect for God extends to His Word, the Bible. The church itself exists to uphold and defend the truth of Scripture, as Paul describes in 1 Timothy 3:15 the pillar and support of the truth. This truth is found solely in God's eternal Word. As a pillar, the church supports the truth by teaching it faithfully. As a foundation, the church upholds the truth by defending it and passing it on to future generations. Our commitment to being a biblical church is rooted in a high view of Scripture. We believe that God has exalted His Name and His Word above all things. A high view of Scripture means we believe and teach several crucial attributes of God's Word.


Inspiration

According to 2 Timothy 3:16, all Scripture is 'God-breathed,' signifying that it is of divine origin. Every word of Scripture is the product of God's inspiration.


Relevancy

In 2 Timothy 3:16, Paul also insists that all Scripture is profitable, useful, and beneficial. We don’t make the Bible relevant—it simply is relevant! Since God has breathed out His words to us, how could anything be more relevant?


Sufficiency

In 2 Timothy 3:15, Paul describes Scripture as “able to make you wise unto salvation.” In verse 17, he adds that the Scripture is sufficient to make us adequate, which means “capable, proficient, able to meet all demands.” And Scripture fully equips us for every good work—it completely outfits us for spiritual service. It is a sufficient resource for the Spirit for our salvation and sanctification.


Authority

Paul, in 2 Timothy 4:1-2, commands pastors and elders to 'preach the Word,' employing the Greek term that signifies a formal and authoritative proclamation, similar to the message of a royal herald. Biblical preaching is not merely a conversation but a proclamation of God's message that demands to be heard and obeyed.


Our church services are centered on Scripture.

Per Paul's instructions in 1 Timothy 4:13-16, the primary focus of our church services is the exposition of Scripture. This emphasis reflects the conviction of the Reformers that the primary purpose of church gatherings is the preaching and teaching of God's Word. We believe that when Scripture is proclaimed, God speaks to His people.


Scripture alone directs and informs the elements of our worship.

We adhere to the Regulative Principle of Worship, which asserts that only those forms of worship explicitly commanded or sanctioned by Scripture are acceptable. This principle stems from Sola Scriptura, the belief that the Bible is the sole and supreme authority for faith and practice. The Second Commandment warns against the dangers of worshiping God in ways not prescribed by Him. The Regulative Principle asks: 'Does Scripture command or sanction this practice?' If not, it is not permissible in our corporate worship. Accordingly, our worship services include the following elements: Singing Scripture-based hymns and songs. Praying in accordance with Scripture. Reading and teaching Scripture. Giving offerings to support the ministry of the church. Observing the ordinances of baptism and the Lord's Supper. These biblically mandated elements lend solemnity to our worship while also providing assurance that we are honoring God through our service.


Expository Preaching

Following the pattern observed in Old Testament worship and in the ministry of Jesus, we prioritize the systematic and consecutive exposition of Scripture in our services. Reading and explaining the Law and Prophets in Old Testament worship was a consistent practice. Jesus Himself actively participated in synagogue services centered on the reading and exposition of God's Word. Similarly, Paul instructed Timothy to prioritize reading, explaining, and applying Scripture during church gatherings. This consistent and systematic teaching pattern of God's Word is evident throughout Scripture, from the Old Testament to the New Testament epistles.


Hermeneutics

We believe every biblical text has one unchanging meaning, determined by the human author's intended meaning and guided by the Holy Spirit. This meaning is conveyed through the text's words, grammar, and literary context. While understanding Scripture can sometimes be challenging, we seek to interpret it accurately through careful exegesis. This involves examining the text's historical and literary context, considering grammar, vocabulary, and cultural background factors. We interpret Scripture literally, meaning we strive to understand the text in its most natural and straightforward sense while recognizing the use of literary devices such as figures of speech, allegories, and symbols. However, we always seek to interpret the text in a way consistent with the author's intended meaning and the overall context.


Contemporary Applications

A high view of Scripture necessitates that we adhere to its teachings on all matters, even those that may be controversial or countercultural. We recognize that our understanding of Scripture may differ from secular and theological prevailing views. The following is not an exhaustive list but rather examples of how a high view of Scripture should shape our thinking on contemporary matters.


A Changed Life

We believe that those who have been saved by God's grace through faith in Christ are transformed into new creations. This transformation is evident in their submission to Christ and obedience to God's Word. While all Christians continue to struggle with sin, even at times significantly, a genuine Christian life is characterized by a progressive decrease in sin and a growing pursuit of holiness.


Creation

We affirm that Genesis presents a literal and historical account of God's creation in six days. We teach, therefore, that God created everything in six literal days. We reject theistic evolution and other interpretations that undermine the historical accuracy of the creation account.


The Gift of Tongues

The gift of tongues, as described in the New Testament, refers to the miraculous ability to speak in unknown languages, enabling the proclamation of the Gospel to diverse audiences. This was a unique manifestation of God's power during the early church, serving to authenticate the Apostles' message. We do not believe that contemporary experiences of 'speaking in tongues' align with the biblical description of this gift. These experiences often involve ecstatic utterances that lack intelligible meaning and do not fulfill the purposes outlined in Scripture.


Human Sexuality

We believe God created humanity in His image, male and female. This reflects the inherent distinction between the sexes established by God at creation. We understand gender to be determined by biological sex at birth. We believe that God designed sexual intimacy to be expressed within the context of marriage between one man and one woman.


Local Church and Parachurch Partnerships

There are two primary reasons parachurch ministries have arisen: 1) Local churches have failed to do what God has commanded, and 2) They specialize in areas that may be beyond the scope or capacity of local churches. Involvement in the local church must take priority over parachurch ministry involvement. Active participation in a local church is essential for spiritual growth and discipleship and is mandated in Scripture, while involvement in parachurch ministry is optional. We also believe those involved in parachurch ministry should be accountable to the leadership of a local church and must operate according to the Word of God.


Marriage

We believe marriage, as a gift of God's common grace, is a foundational societal institution. As the Creator of marriage, God alone defines its nature and purpose, as revealed in His Word. Scripture teaches that marriage is a public, lifelong covenant between one man and one woman. Divorce is permitted only in specific circumstances outlined in Scripture, such as unrepentant sexual immorality or abandonment by an unbeliever. Sexual sin, whether in thought or deed, is a serious transgression against God. God intends for the marriage of believers to reflect the loving relationship between Christ and His church, nurtured through obedience to God's Word and empowered by the Holy Spirit.


Psychology and Psychiatry

The mental health fields of modern Psychology and Psychiatry were established by secular practitioners who often hated God. God’s mercy and grace are sufficient to help people deal with all spiritual problems and sinfulness. While psychiatric and psychological practices may correctly identify and classify spiritual difficulties, they don't provide scripture-based remedies and are inadequate for addressing spiritual concerns. The field of psychology often misclassifies sins as medical conditions and interprets sin-based bondage as addiction. The mental health field encompasses over a hundred distinct and often conflicting theoretical approaches to treating spiritual challenges. Time has demonstrated that psychology and psychiatry are ineffective disciplines that fail to deliver biblical answers to humanity's fundamental spiritual requirements.


Sin and Redemption

Sin is rebellion against God, a violation of His holy character and law, which separates humanity from Him. The Bible declares that all have sinned and fallen short of the glory of God. The penalty for sin is death—eternal separation from God in hell. However, God, in His mercy and love, provided redemption through the sacrificial death and resurrection of His Son, Jesus Christ. Through repentance and faith in Christ, sinners are forgiven, reconciled to God, and granted eternal life.


The Role of Women in Ministry

We affirm the equal worth of men and women as image-bearers of God. Scripture teaches distinct roles for men and women in the home and the church. We believe that while women are gifted and equipped by God to serve in various capacities, the office of elder is reserved for men as outlined in the New Testament. Women are encouraged to teach and disciple other women and children and contribute meaningfully to the ministry of the local church under the leadership of the elders.


Social Justice and the Gospel

The Gospel of Jesus Christ addresses humanity's ultimate need—reconciliation with God through the forgiveness of sins. While Scripture calls us to care for the poor, uphold justice, and love our neighbors, we reject the notion that the church's primary mission is social activism. Social justice concerns, though important, must not overshadow or replace the proclamation of the Gospel. We are committed to preaching Christ crucified as the only hope for a sinful world while encouraging believers to reflect God's justice and mercy in their actions.


Sanctity of Life

We believe that life begins at conception and is a sacred gift from God. Every human being, from the moment of conception, bears the image of God and possesses intrinsic dignity and value. We oppose abortion, euthanasia, and any actions that devalue human life. As Christians, we are called to defend the unborn, care for the vulnerable, and uphold the sanctity of life in all its stages.


The End Times

We believe in the personal, visible, and bodily return of Jesus Christ to establish His kingdom. While there are differing interpretations of the timing and details of eschatological events, we affirm that Christ's return will bring final judgment for the unrighteous and eternal blessing for the redeemed. Believers are called to live in anticipation of His coming, remaining steadfast in faith and committed to fulfilling the Great Commission until He returns.


Biblical Counseling

We believe the Bible is sufficient to address all spiritual and relational issues. Biblical counseling involves applying God's Word to the challenges of life, helping believers grow in their faith and obedience to Christ. This counseling approach recognizes that true change occurs through the power of the Holy Spirit and the renewal of the mind by Scripture. We reject secular counseling theories that contradict or undermine biblical principles.


Church Discipline

Church discipline is a biblical process designed to restore sinning believers, protect the purity of the church, and honor God's holiness. It involves confronting sin with the goal of repentance and reconciliation. If a member persists in unrepentant sin, the church is commanded to pursue corrective discipline, which may include public rebuke and, if necessary, removal from fellowship. This process must always be carried out in love, humility, and adherence to Scripture.


Giving and Stewardship

Christians are called to honor God with their finances, recognizing that all resources ultimately belong to Him. Giving should be an act of worship, done cheerfully, sacrificially, and in proportion to God's provision. Stewardship extends beyond financial giving to include the responsible use of time, talents, and other resources for God's glory and the advancement of His kingdom.


Evangelism and Missions

The Great Commission calls every believer to proclaim the Gospel, making disciples of all nations. Evangelism involves sharing the good news of Jesus Christ with boldness and compassion. Missions extend this mandate by seeking to reach unreached people groups, planting churches, and equipping indigenous leaders. We affirm that the local church is central to the work of evangelism and missions, providing prayer, resources, and accountability for those engaged in these efforts.


The Role of the Holy Spirit

The Holy Spirit indwells every believer at the moment of salvation, empowering them to live godly lives and serve in the church. He convicts the world of sin, righteousness, and judgment, illuminates Scripture, and enables believers to grow in Christlikeness. While we affirm the Spirit's ongoing work in the church, we reject practices that misrepresent His ministry, such as unbiblical manifestations and sensationalism.


Spiritual Gifts

We believe spiritual gifts are sovereignly given by the Holy Spirit for the edification of the church and the glory of God. While some gifts were foundational and temporary (e.g., apostleship, prophecy, and tongues), others remain operative today. These include teaching, service, leadership, mercy, and evangelism. All gifts must be exercised in accordance with Scripture and in a manner that promotes unity and maturity in the church.


Marriage and Family

Marriage is a covenantal union instituted by God, designed to reflect the relationship between Christ and His church. It is exclusively between one man and one woman, intended to be a lifelong commitment. The family is the foundational unit of society, where parents are called to raise their children in the discipline and instruction of the Lord. We affirm the sanctity of marriage and family as essential to God's design for human flourishing.


Human Sexuality

We believe that God created humanity as male and female, distinct and complementary, and that gender is a gift from God. Sexual intimacy is reserved for the covenant of marriage between one man and one woman. Any deviation from this design, including fornication, adultery, homosexuality, and gender reassignment, is contrary to God's Word. We call all people to repentance and faith in Christ, offering the hope of redemption and restoration through the Gospel.


Baptism and the Lord's Supper

Baptism and the Lord's Supper are the two ordinances instituted by Christ for the church. Baptism is an outward expression of an inward faith, symbolizing the believer's union with Christ in His death, burial, and resurrection. It is reserved for those who have professed faith in Christ. The Lord's Supper is a commemoration of Christ's sacrifice, a proclamation of His death, and a reminder of His return. Both ordinances are acts of obedience and worship, to be practiced regularly by the church.


The Local Church

The local church is a community of baptized believers committed to worship, discipleship, fellowship, and mission. It is governed by a plurality of elders and served by deacons, with Christ as the head. The church gathers regularly for the preaching of the Word, the administration of the ordinances, and corporate prayer and worship. Membership in a local church is both a privilege and a responsibility, providing believers with spiritual accountability and opportunities for service.


Christian Liberty

Christian liberty is the freedom believers have in Christ, guided by Scripture and the conscience illuminated by the Holy Spirit. This liberty must never be used as an excuse for sin or a stumbling block to others. In matters not explicitly addressed by Scripture, believers are called to exercise discernment, considering the glory of God, the edification of others, and their own spiritual growth.


Prayer and Worship

Prayer is a vital aspect of the believer's relationship with God, expressing dependence, worship, and intercession. Worship encompasses all aspects of life, as believers are called to glorify God in everything they do. Corporate worship, marked by reverence and joy, includes the singing of psalms, hymns, and spiritual songs, the reading and preaching of Scripture, and the administration of the ordinances. True worship is God-centered, Spirit-led, and grounded in truth.


Christian Unity

The unity of believers is a testimony to the Gospel and a reflection of the oneness of the Godhead. While doctrinal differences exist among Christians, we affirm the essential truths of the faith as the basis for fellowship. We are called to pursue unity without compromising biblical truth, showing love and humility toward one another. Division that arises from sin or false teaching must be addressed in accordance with Scripture.


Cultural Engagement

Christians are called to be salt and light in the world, engaging culture with wisdom and discernment. This involves standing for truth, promoting justice, and demonstrating love and grace in all interactions. While we are to be in the world, we must not conform to its patterns, but rather seek to transform it through the Gospel. Cultural engagement must always prioritize the glory of God and the advancement of His kingdom.


Suffering and Persecution

Suffering is an inevitable part of the Christian life, used by God to refine and strengthen the faith of believers. Persecution for righteousness' sake is to be expected, as the world is hostile to the Gospel. In the face of suffering and persecution, Christians are called to endure with joy, trusting in God's sovereignty and grace. We look forward to the eternal glory that far outweighs the temporary trials of this life.


Eternal Destiny

Every person will spend eternity in one of two destinations: heaven or hell. Heaven is the eternal dwelling place of the redeemed, characterized by perfect communion with God and the absence of sin and suffering. Hell is the eternal punishment for the unrighteous, a place of conscious torment and separation from God's presence. The Gospel offers salvation to all who repent and believe in Christ, securing their place in the kingdom of God.

Every believer is called to be a disciple and to disciple others, fostering spiritual maturity and accountability. Growth in the Christian life is marked by the fruit of the Spirit, a deepening love for God and neighbor, and a commitment to live out the Gospel in every area of life.

The Great Commission

The Great Commission is Christ's mandate to His followers to make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, and teaching them to obey all that He has commanded. This mission involves proclaiming the Gospel, planting churches, and demonstrating the love of Christ through acts of service and compassion. Every believer is called to participate in the Great Commission, whether through prayer, giving, or going to the nations.


Stewardship and Generosity

Stewardship is the responsible management of all that God has entrusted to us, including time, talents, and treasures. Believers are called to be generous, recognizing that everything belongs to God and is to be used for His glory. Giving is an act of worship and a tangible expression of faith, supporting the work of the church and the advancement of the Gospel. Wise stewardship reflects a heart set on eternal treasures rather than earthly possessions.


The Return of Christ

We affirm the imminent and visible return of Jesus Christ, who will come in glory to judge the living and the dead. His return will bring the fulfillment of God’s redemptive plan, the resurrection of the body, and the restoration of all things. Believers are called to live in anticipation of His coming, remaining faithful and steadfast in their walk with the Lord. The return of Christ is the blessed hope of the church and the culmination of history.


The New Creation

God will create a new heaven and a new earth, where righteousness dwells and His glory is fully revealed. The redeemed will dwell with God forever in perfect joy, worship, and communion. The new creation will be free from sin, suffering, and death, a place of eternal peace and flourishing. This eternal state is the ultimate fulfillment of God’s promises, the consummation of His kingdom, and the everlasting hope of believers.


The Glory of God

The ultimate purpose of all creation is the glory of God. His glory is revealed in His character, works, and redemptive plan. Believers are called to glorify God in every aspect of their lives, reflecting His greatness and goodness to the world. Worship, obedience, and faithful living are expressions of our devotion to Him. The glory of God is both our highest calling and our greatest joy, as we find our ultimate fulfillment in Him.
