What is the Gospel message of Jesus Christ?

The Gospel, english for euangelion, means literally good news. But, what exactly is the good news? For there to be good news, there must first be some kind of bad news. And the bad news can be summed up very simply. To understand the Gospel, you must understand both the nature of God and the nature of humanity.

The Most High, the Creator of the universe is holy. Often, we think of holiness in terms of moral purity. But in this sense, we mean that God is completely and totally set apart. Unique. Distinct. Extra-ordinary. Atypical. Unusual. Completely and totally in a category by Himself. There is none like Him. He alone is the Sovereign and He has no equal.

3And one called out to another and said, “Holy, Holy, Holy, is Yahweh of hosts; The whole earth is full of His glory.” — Isaiah 6:3

He is righteous. Everything that comes forth from Him is without blemish. His ways are above our ways and unstained by sin. Further, because of His righteousness, He must judge the evildoer.

"My shield is with God, Who saves the upright in heart. 11God is a righteous judge, And a God who has indignation every day." — Psalm 7:10-11

So, what's the bad news here? Simply put, God is a good God. Wait. How is that bad news? Isn't this a good thing? That God is good? At face value, this sounds like good news in and of itself. But this is only half of the equation. What about the nature of man?

The apostle Paul writes in his letter to the Romans, the following

“There is none righteous, not even one; 11There is none who understands, There is none who seeks for God; 12All have turned aside, together they have become worthless; There is none who does good, There is not even one.” 13“Their throat is an open tomb, With their tongues they keep deceiving,” “The poison of asps is under their lips”; 14“Whose mouth is full of cursing and bitterness”; 15“Their feet are swift to shed blood, 16Destruction and misery are in their paths, 17And the path of peace they have not known.” 18“There is no fear of God before their eyes.”

We read here that gravity of our sinful nature as human beings is much deeper than we realize. Not only are we sinful, but in our fallen condition we can't even do anything good in His eyes. The apostle Paul, formerly a Pharisee, compares our throats to open graves, our tongues to that of a poisonous snake, and our feet to that of a murderer running swiftly to shed blood.

The point here is simple: from head to toe, surface to core, our lives are contaminated by sin. Like a water bottle with a drop of red-dye, so becomes our lives. The dye permeates and contimates the entirity of the bottle, leaving no particle unstained. That is our spiritual condition

Worse yet. These things Paul has listed were things that would make you unclean in Jewish ceremonial law. He is, in effect, calling us as human beings unclean because of our sin.

So the question is, what does a holy, just, and righteous God do with sinners like us, totally stained by sin and living in active rebellion against Him? It is the obligation of the righteous Judge to condemn sinners. Like those who were unclean in the Old Testament, we are cut off from the presence of God because of sin, and left with nothing other than a guilty verdict and a hastily approaching court date.

Only when you understand the nature of God and the nature of humanity can you truly understand the Gospel, the good news of Jesus Christ.

23 for all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God, 24 being justified as a gift by His grace through the redemption which is in Christ Jesus; 25 whom God displayed publicly as a propitiation in His blood through faith, for a demonstration of His righteousness, because in the forbearance of God He passed over the sins previously committed; — Romans 3:23-25 (LSB)

At the cross, the most costly transaction in all history was paid. God made Him, Christ Jesus, who knew no sin, to become sin on our behalf, that we might become the righteousness of God. The penalty that was due you and I was placed on Him at Calvary. The Father's cup of wrath, seen in numerous occassions throughout the Old Testament, was removed out of the hands of His people and placed into the hands of His Son.

This is the good news. That, at the cross, the sins of those who belong to Him were paid in full and the perfectly righteous life of Christ was credited to our behalf, so that someday we can stand blameless before a holy, just, and righteous God.

If you have not turned from sin and believed unto salvation upon the name of Christ, cry out to Him. He alone has the power to save. No amount of good works will save you. Those that turn from sin and place their trust totally on Christ Jesus have a sure and steadfast anchor of salvation.

by John Macarthur
